01The thesis

Nnọọ is an archive. Social is how we let the diaspora in.

The product itself is two hands of one body — a preservation archive (Ọbá) and a learning app — but a brand-new mission can't reach those two audiences cold. Social is the third hand, and its job is small and specific: let people meet an elder, hear a word, sit with a proverb, and then decide whether they want to step closer.

Three audiences, one feed. We are not selling to any of them.

Elders & potential facilitators see we are serious, that names are credited, that recordings stay with their communities. Diaspora learners see that Igbo is alive in particular hands and mouths, not on a flash-card. Funders & press see the work moving — milestones, methodology, real recordings.

Every post should make at least one of those audiences feel welcome home. If a post doesn't, it should not ship.

We are starting with one promise: every recording we keep, we keep with the elder's name and the elder's permission. No exceptions. Pinned post · everywhere

Why this matters

The brand is the methodology.

Every other Igbo-learning resource on social either gamifies the language (slick, hollow) or memorializes it (reverent, stuck). Our register sits between those — warm, slow, specific. That register is the differentiator, not the colors.

03Voice & tone

One voice. Three registers, depending on who's listening.

The brand voice is reverent & warm, with moments of encouragement. But registers shift by pillar: an elder spotlight is quiet; a word-of-the-day is encouraging; a call to facilitators is direct. Same voice, different volume.

PillarRegisterIn our voiceNot our voice
Daily proverb (ilu) Reverent & quiet — museum register. Igbo first, translation in italics, gloss in muted prose. Attribution to a dialect when known. "💡 Wisdom Wednesday! 🌍 #African"
Word of the day Encouraging & teacherly. "ụbọchị — day. Often used in greetings. Try saying Ụbọchị ọma today." "Slay your Igbo with this word 🔥"
Elder spotlight Reverent. Name, dialect, place. Always. "Mama Adaeze, b. 1942, Nri. Recorded 14 stories over three afternoons." "Meet our amazing storyteller!" (no name, no place)
Hometown stories Familial & specific. Plum register. "Awka has 53 recordings now. Looking for ndị Awka now living abroad." "From Nigeria with love 🇳🇬"
Behind the archive Honest field notes. First-person facilitator. "We meant to record the kola-nut words first. We let him start with the songs instead. Better recordings than we could have planned." "Sneak peek behind the scenes! 👀"
Diaspora voices Warm, named, age-aware. "Chioma, 11, Atlanta. Six weeks in. Her grandma laughed when she said nnà m on the phone." "Real stories from our community 💕"
Culture explainers Educational & specific. Cite linguists, elders. "Eke. Orie. Afọ. Nkwọ. The four market days — and why children are named for them." "5 facts about Igbo culture you didn't know! 🤯"
Product updates Plain & precise. No hype. "v0.4 ships tone-feedback in real time. Six dialects added to Ọbá." "We're SO excited to announce…"
Call to action Direct & specific. Make the ask small. "Do you have an elder at home? An afternoon and a phone is all we need to start." "Donate now to support our mission! 🙏"

The non-negotiables

  • Igbo proper nouns always keep their diacritics — Nnọọ, Ọbá, Ọha, Ndị Igbo. Never strip.
  • Igbo words are not italicized in our copy. They are not foreign here. Roman weight, same as English.
  • Proverbs always show Igbo first, then translation, then gloss. Attribute to a dialect when known.
  • Never "users." Say learners, elders, facilitators, contributors.
  • "We" is the community, not the company.
  • No emoji in chrome or our own copy. Learners may use them; we don't.

Tone calibration cheat-sheet

When you don't know the tone, default to "a patient relative talking to a younger relative" — never to "a brand talking to its audience".

A relative names things. Doesn't oversell. Repeats themselves when it matters. Knows when to stop talking. That's the tone.

04Visual system

The same six colors, three typefaces, and one motif — used differently per pillar.

Don't invent new colors. Don't introduce a fourth typeface. Don't lean on the arcs motif as a watermark on every post — it earns its place when a post is text-heavy and needs warmth.

Palette — what each color does on social

Clay
Brand · default poster bg
Marigold
Accent · "word of day"
Forest
Archive · provenance
Plum
Community · Ọha
Bone
Neutral · proverb cards
Cream
Raised · elder cards

One color per post, used boldly. Don't mix clay + plum + forest on the same card — pick the one that matches the pillar.

Type — three families, three jobs

Outfit

Display, headlines, big Igbo words. 700/800 weight on posters; 600 on cards. Set tight (-0.02em).

DM Sans

Body, captions, eyebrows, CTA labels. Eyebrows are uppercase, +0.12em tracking.

Fraunces — reserved for the archive.

Elder names, proverb translations, recording transcripts. Italic by default. Never used in chrome or CTA labels.

Motif — when to use the arcs

Two arcs meeting a dot — abstract, not a traditional motif. Use as a corner decoration on text-only posters, at 10–18% opacity. Never as a background pattern repeat.

Waveform — the audio signature

Use in archive / elder / audio-clip posts only. Always in clay (or cream-on-clay). Never decorative — it should imply real audio content.

Photo placeholder system

Use tonal color-fields where real elder photos don't yet exist. Label them honestly: "Portrait pending — Mama Adaeze, Nri." Never AI-generate African faces.

06Content pillars

Nine pillars. Each one does a job for at least one audience.

A weekly mix is roughly 40% language-first (proverb + word), 30% archive-first (elder + hometown + behind), 20% community-first (diaspora + culture), 10% product / CTA. Avoid letting product / CTA exceed that — the archive is the ad.

Language

01 · Daily proverb (ilu)

Igbo proverb + literal translation + one-line gloss + dialect attribution. The flagship daily post.

Language

02 · Word of the day

One Igbo word, with phonetic and tone marks called out. Optional audio. Used in a sentence.

Archive

03 · Elder spotlight

Named elder, age, dialect, hometown, recording count. The single most important pillar for funder trust.

Community

04 · Hometown stories

Recordings tied to a state / LGA / village. Milestone counts when they hit. Recruits diaspora by hometown.

Archive

05 · Behind the archive

Field notes from facilitators. Honest, short, first-person. The voice of the methodology.

Community

06 · Diaspora voices

Named learner quotes, ages, where they live, what shifted. Always with permission.

Education

07 · Culture explainers

Carousels and long-form. Naming days, kola-nut, tonal grammar, market weeks. Cite sources.

Product

08 · Product / app updates

Feature releases & milestones. Plain copy, no hype. Cap at ~1 per fortnight.

Conversion

09 · Call to action

Three flavors — recruit facilitators, ask for donations, drive app downloads. Specific asks only.

09Captions & hashtags

The caption is half the post. It's also the place we get sloppy fastest.

Captions follow a four-line shape on Instagram & Facebook, a three-line shape on X, and a two-line shape on TikTok. Hashtags live below a divider, never inline.

Caption formula — Instagram

[Igbo word or proverb — line break]
[English translation in plain prose.]

[One specific context line — a person, a place,
 a moment when this might be said.]

[Soft CTA — "Hear it on Ọbá", "Listen at the link",
 "Become a facilitator →"]
————
#Nnoo #IgboLanguage #NdiIgbo
#[hometown LGA] #[dialect] #Diaspora

Caption formula — X / Twitter

[Igbo line — bold, on its own.]

[Translation in italic-feeling prose.]

— [Attribution: elder name · dialect · place]
[Listen URL]

No hashtag stack. Keep one (#Nnoo) at the very end only if discovery matters that day. Thread for context, don't cram it into the post.

Hashtag stack — always

#Nnoo · #IgboLanguage · #NdiIgbo — the brand stack. Use on every post.

Hashtag stack — by pillar

#Ilu for proverbs · #Okwu for words · #Oba for archive · #Oha for community · #Diaspora for learners. Pick one per post.

Hashtag stack — by place

Always tag the relevant LGA & state when a post is hometown-anchored: #Anambra · #NnewiNorth · #Awka. This is where hometown unions find us.

Attribution — non-negotiable

Every elder appearance includes a name, year of birth (or estimate), LGA, and dialect. Every learner appearance includes name, age, and city, and for minors, "shared with permission of [parent/guardian name]" sits in the caption. Every proverb cites a dialect / region when known; if unknown, say so.

If you don't have an attribution, the post is not ready. There is no "use today, fix later."

Per-month additions

One milestone post when a number crosses (recordings / dialects / hometowns). One product update when something ships. One donation post — never more.

11Guardrails

Do, don't, and why.

If you can only memorize one section of this playbook, memorize this one.

TopicDoDon'tWhy
Diacritics Nnọọ. Ọbá. Ọha. Ndị Igbo. Nnoo. Oba. Oha. Ndi Igbo. The diacritics are content, not decoration. Stripping them is the difference between language preservation and language flattening.
Emoji in chrome Use language. "Daalụ." "Welcome home." 🇳🇬 🙏 ✨ 💕 Emoji flag-and-feel posts read as generic Africa branding. The brand carries warmth through color and word.
Photography of elders Specific, named, golden-hour, dignified. Stock "African elder" photos · drums-in-silhouette · safari sunsets. If we don't have a real photo, the tonal placeholder is honest. A stock photo is not.
AI image gen for people Use tonal placeholders + "portrait pending" label. Generate African faces with AI, even temporarily. This is a preservation project. AI-generated likenesses of our subject community would be a defining brand violation.
Traditional motifs Use our abstract two-arcs motif only. Slather uli, nsibidi, akwete patterns on chrome. Those belong to specific communities and artists. Sourcing from them with attribution + compensation is its own future workstream.
Italicizing Igbo Set Igbo in roman, same weight as English. Italicize Igbo words as if they were foreign. They aren't foreign here. The italic register belongs to translation, not the source language.
Proverb handling Igbo first · literal translation · gloss · attribution. Decorate proverbs as inspirational quotes. A proverb is not chrome. Don't slap them over sunset photos.
Streak / gamification copy "Welcome back. The words waited for you." "You lost your 14-day streak! 🔥 Come back!" We are not Duolingo. Pressure-coded copy is out of register.
"Users" / "consumers" Learners. Elders. Facilitators. Contributors. Users. Audience. Consumers. Eyeballs. This is a community, not a market.
Cross-posting Reformat for each platform's native shape & voice. Auto-cross-post the same square + caption everywhere. Cross-posted square IG posts on TikTok & X look careless. 30 minutes of reformatting per post saves the brand.
12Governance

What to settle with the community before any of this ships.

This playbook is a scaffold, not a final identity. A handful of decisions belong to Igbo elders, artists, and community organizers — not to us.

Open questions to resolve

  1. An elder council review. Voice, naming, the "we" pronoun, the way we handle dialect spellings — review with at least three elders from three states before launch.
  2. Visual co-authorship. Replace the two-arcs motif (abstract, placeholder) with a commissioned mark by an Igbo artist as soon as budget allows.
  3. Hometown union outreach. Before promoting hometown content from a specific LGA, contact the relevant union for blessing and to credit them.
  4. Dialect representation. No dialect ranking. Be transparent about which dialects are over- or under-represented and why.
  5. Recording consent & ownership. Every elder must have countersigned consent that names them as co-owner of their recording. Surface this on profile bio + pinned.

The standard to hold ourselves to
