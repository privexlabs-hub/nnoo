Nnọọ — business brief

A voice-first platform that records the living culture of Ndị Igbo from elders, lets their own hometown decide what is kept, and teaches it to the diaspora in the elders' own voices, with tonal feedback. Two hands, one body.

01Executive summary

Nnọọ (Igbo: welcome) is built as two hands of one body:

  • Aka nri — the right hand: learning. Five-minute daily lessons where a named elder says each word first, the learner repeats it, and the app shows their pitch against the elder's so they can match the tones that change meaning.
  • Aka ekpe — the left hand: the archive (Ọbá). Community facilitators record elders telling stories, proverbs, songs, recipes, crafts and place-lore, with consent, provenance and payment to the elder.

Two community layers join the hands. Ikpe (the council) is where kin from the elder's hometown vote on every new recording before it enters the archive — "Igbo enwe eze", the Igbo have no king. Ọha (the gathering) connects diaspora Igbo by hometown, shows where kin live, and organises the next recordings.

The bet: the diaspora wants to learn from its own people, not a generic app. If the community owns the archive and decides what goes into it, it will help build it, trust it and pay for it.

02The problem

Language is passing faster than it is being passed on

Igbo is spoken by tens of millions of people, yet many urban and diaspora children grow up knowing a few greetings and little else. The deepest knowledge — dialect, proverb, ceremony, local history — sits with elders and is rarely recorded in a form the next generation can use. Why tone and trust both matter

Igbo is tonal — the same letters carry different meanings at different pitches — so learners must hear real speakers and get feedback on their own voice. And because this is a community's knowledge, communities must control what is recorded, who hears it and how it is credited. Nnọọ is designed around both.

06Consent tiers

Every recording carries one of three tiers, shown as a coloured badge everywhere it appears. The elder chooses. The model follows established Indigenous-archive practice (Mukurtu CMS and Local Contexts Traditional Knowledge Labels).

PublicỌhaAnyone may listen. Used for most lessons, proverbs and stories.
Kin onlyUmunnaOnly people from the same hometown may listen — e.g. a lineage song.
SacredNsọThe record is kept (title, place, length, contributor, custodian) but the sound is not hosted. Initiated members can ask a custodian to open it. "Not a paywall — a threshold."

Ikpe — the council

"Igbo enwe eze." — The Igbo have no king.

08How each person uses it

Learner — a day with Nnọọ

  1. Downloads the app or opens the web app; sets their hometown and dialect.
  2. Is greeted "Nnọọ, [name]" and sees today's lesson, the market day and the proverb.
  3. Hears the elder say each word, sees the tone shape, speaks, gets "Fọdụrụ ntakịrị" or "Ọ dị mma", and tries again if needed.
  4. Opens the proverb and reveals translation and meaning at their own pace.
  5. Listens in Ọbá to recordings from their hometown and saves words to keep close.
  6. Answers the Ikpe prompt if a recording from their town is waiting.
  7. Joins their hometown circle in Ọha, finds kin nearby, and may sponsor or nominate the next recording.

Facilitator — a recording session

  1. Signs up from You or through a hometown union; completes training.
  2. Visits the elder with family; ticks quiet room and mic level.
  3. Explains ownership; the elder chooses the consent tier.
  4. Picks a topic with the elder and records at the elder's pace, adding notes.
  5. Reviews playback, tags and provenance, and confirms the payment due on pass.
  6. Sends to Ikpe and follows the case; answers kin questions or records more if they vote Wait.
  7. Is credited in the provenance chain when it passes.

Kin voter — deciding in Ikpe

  1. Sees the badge: a recording from their hometown is waiting.
  2. Listens to the whole recording.
  3. Reads the four questions, and the comments from kin and elders.
  4. Votes Keep, Wait or No; may comment; can change the vote until the case closes.
  5. Watches the quorum fill; sees the result when the case closes.

Elder — sharing knowledge

  1. Is invited by family, a facilitator or a hometown union — never cold-approached.
  2. Hears plainly that the recording belongs to them and their community; chooses Ọha, Umunna or Nsọ.
  3. Speaks at their own pace on a subject they choose.
  4. Is paid when Ikpe passes the recording, and named wherever it is used.
  5. May join Ikpe discussions with an Elder badge, and can change the tier or withdraw at any time.

Cultural custodian

  1. Validates recordings for accuracy and appropriateness; their name and date appear in the provenance chain.
  2. Holds Nsọ material for their community and handles "request path to custodian" enquiries.
  3. Verifies initiated members and opens access where tradition allows.

Community organiser

  1. Hosts an Ọha circle for a hometown or city.
  2. Posts a sponsored session with a named elder and collects contributions.
  3. Coordinates with a facilitator; the resulting recording goes through Ikpe like any other.

Parent / family

  1. Arrives through the marketing site ("Begin, free") or a hometown circle.
  2. Sets up children's learning with the family dialect Proposed family plan.
  3. Arranges for a grandparent to be recorded so the children learn in their own family's voice.

13Operations

Content pipeline

  1. Facilitator records with consent tier and metadata; sends to Ikpe.
  2. Transcription in the dialect and English translation.
  3. Cultural custodian validates; Nsọ material is metadata-only from the start.
  4. Ikpe: kin listen, comment and vote until quorum and majority (usually 2–4 days).
  5. Passed → live in Ọbá at its tier; elder paid; provenance chain completed. Wait → back to the facilitator with the comments.
  6. Suitable Ọha-tier material becomes lessons and social posts, always credited.

Team roles (initial)

Director · product and engineering lead · linguist and content lead · community and facilitator lead · custodian liaison · social and brand manager · part-time transcribers by dialect. Validate

14Ethics and governance

  • Consent tiers — the elder chooses Ọha, Umunna or Nsọ, and can change or withdraw at any time.
  • Ikpe — nothing is published without passing the kin council. Staff never overrule a council "No".
  • Nsọ — sacred knowledge is recorded as metadata only; custodians control access.
  • Fair return — every passed recording is paid.
  • Attribution — elders named with town, age and dialect; facilitators and custodians credited.
  • Privacy — appearing on the kin map is optional; personal data kept to a minimum; compliance with Nigeria's data protection law and GDPR. Validate
  • Minors — written guardian permission for any learner story.
  • Cultural IP — no uli, nsibidi or akwete patterns in the brand unless commissioned from Igbo artists.
  • No synthetic likeness — no AI-generated Igbo faces or voices presented as real.
  • Dialect equity — both variants are right; kin can add transcript notes instead of "correcting".
  • Elder advisory council — reviews Ikpe rules, tiers and voice before launch and yearly.

21Onboarding, accounts and verification

Learner sign-up Proposed default

  1. Welcome: "Nnọọ. Welcome home." Options: Begin, free / I already have an account.
  2. Account by email, phone number (important in Nigeria) or Apple/Google.
  3. Choose hometown: search a town, confirm its LGA and state. "I don't know" is allowed — the learner can ask family later.
  4. Choose dialect: suggested from the hometown, changeable.
  5. Microphone permission, explained: "We listen only while you hold the button."
  6. Warm-up word (nnọọ) so the first feedback happens in under a minute.
  7. Land on Today.

Kin verification Proposed default

Anyone can say where they are from, but only verified kin count toward Ikpe quorum and hear Umunna recordings. Verification happens in one of three ways:

  • Vouching — two verified kin from the same hometown confirm the person.
  • Organiser — a hometown union or circle organiser with a Ndụmọdụ badge confirms them.
  • Facilitator — confirmed in person during a recording visit.

Verification can be removed if vouching proves false. A person has one hometown for Ikpe purposes (their paternal or maternal town, their choice); they can list a second for listening only.

Children

Under-13s use a child profile inside a guardian's account. Children never appear in Ọha, cannot post or vote, and never appear on the kin map. Aged 13–17: can learn and read Ọha, but cannot vote until 18. Validate the age limits against the law in each market.

Facilitators and custodians

  • Facilitator: applies from You → completes training (consent, recording, cultural protocol, 4 short modules) → does one supervised practice session → approved by staff → given the Facilitator role.
  • Custodian: nominated by a hometown union or the elder council, confirmed by staff, then assigned to one or more hometowns.

22Ikpe rules

The prototype shows the voting screens; these are the rules behind them. Proposed default throughout — confirm with the elder council.

RuleDefault
Who sees a caseỌha-tier cases: every signed-in member aged 18+. Umunna-tier cases: verified kin of that hometown only. Nsọ: never enters open Ikpe — reviewed by the custodian and 3 initiated elders only.
Who countsOnly verified kin of the recording's hometown count toward kin quorum. Other votes are shown separately as "wider Ọha" and do not decide the outcome.
Kin quorum10% of the hometown's verified kin, at least 10 and at most 40 votes.
Small hometownsIf a town has fewer than 30 verified kin: quorum is 5 kin plus the custodian's approval.
PassKin quorum reached and at least two-thirds of kin votes are Keep.
Return (Wait)If more than a third of kin votes are Wait: the case returns to the facilitator with the comments. Resubmitting resets the votes.
Decline (No)If more than a third of kin votes are No: the case is declined. The elder and facilitator are told privately and kindly. The audio stays with the elder's family, not in Ọbá.
Time limit14 days. If quorum is not reached, the facilitator and hometown organiser are prompted to invite kin; after 28 days the case expires and can be resubmitted.
VotingOne vote per person; changeable until the case closes. Listening to at least 80% of the recording is required before voting.
Conflicts of interestThe facilitator and the elder's immediate family can comment but their votes do not count.
Elder's wishesThe elder can withdraw or restrict at any point; this overrides any vote.
Safety holdStaff can pause a case (never publish it) for legal, safety or consent concerns.

24Money flows

Contributor payments Proposed default

  1. At review, the facilitator confirms the payee (the elder or a named family member) and the payout method.
  2. When Ikpe passes, the payout is queued automatically.
  3. Paid within 7 days by bank transfer or mobile money; for elders without either, cash via the facilitator with a signed or photographed receipt.
  4. Status shows in the provenance chain ("acknowledged") and in the facilitator's sessions list.
  5. Amount: flat rate per passed session (₦15,000 in the prototype), with a higher rate for sessions over 30 minutes. Validate

Subscriptions Proposed default

  • Free: Today, proverb, first unit of lessons, all of Ọbá (Ọha tier), Ikpe and Ọha.
  • Paid (individual or family up to 6): all lessons, full pitch feedback, offline downloads, words kept close.
  • Local pricing for Nigeria; store billing on iOS and Android; card billing on web.
  • Never behind a paywall: the archive, Ikpe, Ọha and consent controls.

Sponsored sessions

Money collected in Ọha is held by Nnọọ (not the organiser) until the session is recorded. If the session doesn't happen within 60 days, contributors are refunded. The elder's payment comes from this pot; any surplus funds the next session in the same hometown. Proposed default

25Learning engine

Curriculum structure

Unit (e.g. "Home and family") → Lesson (5 minutes, 4–8 items) → Item (a word or phrase with the Igbo spelling and tone marks, phonetic guide, English meaning, part of speech, dialect, elder clip, pitch target, optional saying from the elder). Each lesson is written once and voiced in several dialects.

Which elder's voice is used

For each item the app picks a clip in this order: learner's hometown → neighbouring towns → same dialect group → any passed Ọha-tier clip. The elder is always named on screen.

Pitch feedback Proposed default

  • Extract the pitch curve from the learner's and the elder's audio, normalise for voice height (children and men sound different from elders), and compare the shape, not absolute pitch.
  • 85% match or above → "Ọ dị mma". 60–84% → "Fọdụrụ ntakịrị", with the syllable that drifted. Below 60% → "Let's hear her again" and replay the elder.
  • Runs on the device where possible, so it works offline and audio is not uploaded. Learner recordings are not stored unless the learner chooses to save one.
  • Tested against native-speaker judgements for each dialect before launch.

Progress without pressure

Recommended resolution of the streak question: show "days practised this week" everywhere, never a streak that breaks. Returning copy: "Welcome back. The words waited for you." Proposed default

Content needed before launch

At least 3 dialects × 30 lessons (about 180 items each), 365 proverbs with meanings, and the elder recordings to voice them. This is the largest single piece of work in the project.

29Admin console

Not in the prototype, but required to run the service. Staff tools never include a "publish" button — only Ikpe publishes.

  • Verification queue — review vouching, revoke false claims.
  • Facilitators — applications, training progress, approval, suspension.
  • Custodians — appoint, assign hometowns, see their queues.
  • Cases — watch all Ikpe cases, extend deadlines, apply a safety hold.
  • Transcription — assign recordings to transcribers by dialect; review translations.
  • Content — build units and lessons, pick elder clips, schedule proverbs.
  • Payments — payout queue, receipts, sponsored-session funds, refunds.
  • Reports — Ọha posts and comments flagged by members.
  • Consent changes — process withdrawals and tier changes within the promised times.
  • Audit log — every staff action recorded.

30Community safety

  • Short community code: respect elders, no insults about any hometown or dialect, no sharing Umunna or Nsọ content outside Nnọọ, no fundraising outside sponsored sessions.
  • Report on every post, reply and comment; three reports hide it pending review.
  • Organisers can moderate their circle; staff handle appeals.
  • Disagreements about a telling go into transcript notes ("in Nnewi central we usually say…") rather than removal.
  • Screenshots and downloads of Umunna recordings are blocked where the platform allows, with a watermark on playback.
